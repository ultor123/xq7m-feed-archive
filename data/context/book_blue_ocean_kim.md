# Blue Ocean Strategy — W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne
> Distilled for speaking, pitching, and daily insight. Framework notes in own words.

## Core Thesis
Markets come in two colors. **Red oceans** are known, contested space where rivals fight over shrinking share — competition turns the water bloody. **Blue oceans** are uncontested space you *create*, where demand is made rather than fought over and competition is irrelevant because you set the terms. The strategic goal isn't to beat the competition — it's to make it irrelevant.

## Value Innovation (the engine)
The conventional belief is a trade-off: you either differentiate (higher value, higher cost) or lead on cost (lower value, lower price). Blue ocean strategy **breaks the trade-off** — pursue *differentiation and low cost simultaneously*. You do that by raising and creating factors buyers value while eliminating and reducing factors the industry competes on out of habit. Value goes up for the buyer; cost goes down for you.

## The Tools
- **Strategy Canvas** — plot the industry's competing factors on the x-axis and the offering level on the y-axis. Rivals' "value curves" usually overlap (everyone competes on the same things). A blue-ocean curve looks *different*, not just higher.
- **Four Actions Framework (ERRC grid)** — four questions that reconstruct buyer value:
  - **Eliminate** — which factors the industry takes for granted should be removed?
  - **Reduce** — which should be cut well below the standard?
  - **Raise** — which should be lifted well above the standard?
  - **Create** — which factors the industry never offered should be introduced?
- **Three traits of a great strategy:** **Focus** (not spread thin across every factor), **Divergence** (a value curve that departs from the pack), and a **Compelling Tagline** (if you can't say it in one true, sharp line, it's not there yet).

## Finding the Space
- **Six Paths Framework** — look across alternative industries, across strategic groups, across buyer groups (purchaser vs user vs influencer), across complementary offerings, across functional-vs-emotional appeal, and across time (trends).
- **Three Tiers of Noncustomers** — soon-to-be (on the edge, ready to leave), refusing (chose against the market), and unexplored (never considered you). Blue oceans are built by aggregating the demand of noncustomers, not by fighting for existing ones.
- **Sequence:** buyer utility → price (strategic, for the mass) → cost (hit the target via ERRC) → adoption hurdles. In that order.

## Kiln Applications
- **Build a strategy canvas for each client.** Map their category's competing factors, show where every rival's curve overlaps, and design a divergent curve. This is a concrete, sellable GTM deliverable.
- **Use ERRC on the client's own offer** — what can they eliminate/reduce that the category over-invests in, and create that no one offers? That's the wedge for outbound messaging.
- **Target noncustomers in list-building.** The biggest untapped pipeline is often the "unexplored" tier — companies that never considered the category at all.
- **Demand the one-line tagline.** If a client's blue-ocean idea can't be said compellingly in a sentence, the positioning isn't done.
