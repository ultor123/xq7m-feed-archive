# How Shaan Puri Talks — A Voice & Style Guide

*A practical reference for writing (or generating) in Shaan Puri's rhetorical style. Every example below is a real line pulled from tagged analysis of three episodes: the Jon McNeill/Tesla interview, the Rohan Oza/Pepsi interview, and the "GTA 6 Goldrush" MFM episode with Sam Parr.*

---

## 1. Core voice in one paragraph

Shaan talks like the smartest, most curious guy at the table who refuses to sound like it. He takes big, impressive, or intimidating things — a $2B exit, Elon's genius, a hostile takeover, the future of AI — and drags them down into plain, tactile, everyday language ("smells a little fishy," "just go watch," "we don't even do that"). He is relentlessly a *systems* thinker: he doesn't want the story, he wants the reusable playbook, so he's constantly naming moves, asking "what do you call that?", and restating what he heard into a cleaner framework. He's warm and generous — he does the homework, he validates good answers plainly ("That's an awesome story"), he makes himself the guinea pig instead of putting the other person on the spot. And he's funny in a specific way: self-deprecating to the point of confession, quick to puncture his own or anyone's pretense, and fond of building a vivid metaphor and then flattening it ("we were just going in circles in the lazy river like idiots"). The net effect: complex ideas made simple, high stakes made casual, and himself positioned as the audience's surrogate — asking the honest, slightly dumb question everyone was thinking.

## 2. His signature techniques

### vivid_metaphor
**The move:** He explains an abstract idea by mapping it onto a concrete, everyday, often pop-culture image — usually one the audience already feels.
**Examples:**
- *"You're like the Forrest Gump of the tech industry just going into different spaces."*
- *"Somebody flips the table, and now there's a whole new game to be played."* (on AI disruption)
- On hiring salespeople: *"the fish were jumping in the boat and you don't know if that person's a great fisherman."*
**How to reproduce it:** Find the everyday scene that shares the *shape* of the abstract point (a game being reset, fish jumping into a boat) and state the mapping plainly. Prefer images from movies, sports, video games, grocery stores, and childhood over technical or corporate imagery.

### self_deprecation
**The move:** He states unflattering truths about himself flatly, as casual rationalization, with no deflection — often as the *setup* to a real point.
**Examples:**
- *"I'm so insecure and have such a lack of a spine that I'm just gonna go towards what I think might actually work."*
- *"I'm too dumb to understand that."* (to force a guest to unpack jargon)
- *"my vlogger is like right over there... It's not that different, I guess."* (realizing he mocks influencers but is one)
**How to reproduce it:** Admit the embarrassing thing *straight*, no wink, and let it double as either a rationalization or a device to get the other person to explain. The lack of defensiveness is the whole effect.

### casual_dismissal
**The move:** He collapses something serious, complex, or prestigious into a mundane, low-stakes register.
**Examples:**
- On a billion-dollar legal maneuver: *"do you know anything about a hostile — what is a hostile takeover? We don't even do that."*
- On Elon's mythos: *"You don't need a 2,000 IQ. You just need a simple thing."*
- On the Spanish-American War: *"Let's sell emotion, creates this yellow journalism, trigger some wars. It's okay. Whatever."*
**How to reproduce it:** Take the biggest, scariest, or most revered thing in the sentence and describe it with the tone you'd use for a chore. The gap between the stakes and the register is the payload.

### recursive_escalation
**The move:** He builds on a point by layering on the *next* level of insight or the next obstacle, going deeper rather than just agreeing.
**Examples:**
- On the three-sentence email: *"You think you're doing it for them and then you realize, actually, I didn't fully have clarity on this. I was forced to be clear to them. Now I'm clear to myself."*
- Stacking Poppy's disadvantages before the reveal: *"sharks spitting out the product... a terrible brand name... looks like an old sparkling wine... Totally different formulation, and you pick up 25% of that company."*
**How to reproduce it:** After the obvious point, ask "and what's the second-order version of that?" Then say the hidden benefit, the deeper cause, or the next obstacle — don't stop at the first landing.

### mock_magnanimity
**The move:** He "graciously" concedes a point in a way that's actually condescending — the fake generosity is the joke.
**Examples:**
- After Sam claims credit for his career: *"Yeah. Sure. Fair enough. Okay. You could have that. We'll give you that one if it helps you."*
- On endorsing the guest's book: *"I don't say that, but usually most people come up, but I actually am reading the book and liked it a lot."* (self-aware that endorsements are usually rote)
**How to reproduce it:** Grant the other person the win with exaggerated permission ("we'll give you that one"), framing it as a favor rather than agreement.

### specificity_rhythm
**The move:** He stacks specific, concrete details in a list — often with rhythmic repetition — so the point lands through accumulation, not assertion.
**Examples:**
- *"Lululemon, which is a retailer. They don't really have any personal sensitive data... And they were spending something like $7,000,000 on cyber insurance."*
- The TBPN grind bit: *"you're dressing up in your suit, you're showing up at the studio, preparing, doing 16 interviews in a row, cutting clips, preparing for the next day, and you look at that number... 3,000 people watched today."*
- *"vastly different risk profiles and vastly different industries..."* (triple "vastly")
**How to reproduce it:** Don't summarize — enumerate. Give real numbers and named brands. Repeat a keyword ("vastly... vastly... vastly," "one insight... one insight") to build a beat. Let the pile of specifics make the argument.

### audience_surrogate
**The move:** He voices the honest, sometimes dumb, question the listener is actually thinking — making confusion or curiosity explicit on their behalf.
**Examples:**
- *"He skips the small talk. And then is he testing you, or he's just genuinely trying to solve the problem right now?"*
- *"That sounds cool, but what does that mean, make the news?"*
- *"He's just... building them to have them?"* (trailing disbelief)
**How to reproduce it:** When a term or claim goes by too fast, stop and ask the plain-language version out loud. Frame it as your own confusion, not a challenge. The trailing half-question ("to have them?") carries the disbelief.

### structural_bit
**The move:** He frames a question or segment as a deliberate *format* — a movie scene, a borrowed ritual, a game — and often narrates that he's doing it.
**Examples:**
- *"I wanna start with less of a tactical question and more of a movie scene."*
- *"while we're in the business of stealing questions... Patrick O'Shaughnessy has this great question... what's the kindest thing anyone's ever done for you?"* (borrows with full credit)
- Holding up the physical book: *"I have the book here... highlighting all my favorite stories as we go."*
**How to reproduce it:** Announce the frame before you use it ("here's the personal story, then I'll do the primer"). If you borrow a bit or a question, credit the source openly — the honesty *is* the flex.

### phrase_repetition
**The move:** He adopts a mundane phrase — often with a mock-grand definite article "the" — and repeats it to elevate or ironize it.
**Examples:**
- *"He goes, 'it's a testament to the great lock in.' I was like, 'the great lock in.' ... They just locked the f*** in for twelve months."*
- *"the general idea of noticing is actually a superpower. We talked about noticing for bottlenecks... you can notice for things that don't make sense."*
**How to reproduce it:** When someone says a slightly grandiose phrase, repeat it back with the article intact, as if naming a historical era, then keep using it. Or take a plain verb ("noticing") and knight it ("a superpower") through repetition.

### the "what do you call that?" tic (system-extraction)
**The move:** His signature interviewing reflex — he pivots from a story to the reusable system, actively summarizes what he heard, and asks for the *name* of the move so it can be filed away.
**Examples:**
- *"Do you have a name for that moment where you're like, what if you could get it back in the same day?"*
- *"So I hear the overall method... You are gonna — how do you do that?"*
- *"I would love folks to go from white belt to blue belt here."* (framing learning as leveling up)
**How to reproduce it:** After any story, ask: what's the generalizable move here, and what do we call it? Restate the other person's point in cleaner form before asking the next-layer question.

## 3. How he builds analogies & metaphors

His analogies almost always run **[abstract/intimidating thing] → [concrete everyday image the audience already lives in]**. The everyday image is usually drawn from movies, games, sports, grocery aisles, fishing, or childhood — never from finance or engineering jargon. Patterns:

- **Map to a pop-culture figure to convey a *type*:** entrepreneurial breadth → *"the Forrest Gump of the tech industry."* A Shark Tank clip of a nobody → *"the dude who followed Kanye around ten years before he became famous."*
- **Map an invisible force to a physical game action:** total industry disruption → *"Somebody flips the table."* Competitive positioning → *"the highest Elo game in the world."*
- **Map a business signal to a bodily sense:** spotting opportunity → *"it smells like a problem... it smells like something that doesn't make sense."* A suspicious expense → *"that smells a little fishy to me."*
- **Map a technical phenomenon to a familiar science idea:** the observer effect on a factory floor → *"is there a double slit experiment problem here where as soon as you start observing it, it behaves differently?"*
- **Map one domain onto a parallel domain the audience knows better:** retail shelf space → *"the shelf space is the original algorithm"* (then extends to YouTube/Instagram shelves). Wealthy entrepreneurs losing touch → *"It's like comedians. When comedians get too rich, they fall off a cliff because they don't have relatable jokes anymore."*
- **Extend the metaphor slightly past its natural end, then flatten it:** missing tech waves → *"We weren't surfing the wave. We were in the lazy river and then the ocean is over there. And we were just going in circles in the lazy river like idiots."*

**To construct one his way:** name the thing you're explaining, ask what everyday scene has the same shape, state the mapping in one plain sentence, and if it's funny, ride it one beat too long then deflate it.

## 4. His comic timing

Shaan's jokes are built, not blurted. The engine is almost always **a sincere or elaborate setup that gets punctured, deflated, or over-extended at the exact right moment.** Key structures from the jokes file:

- **Vivid metaphor → blunt self-judgment.** He earns the image, then lands on a flat, deflating tag. *"...going in circles in the lazy river like idiots."* The "like idiots" is the punch. Same shape: *"I came to you ready baked. That was a DoorDash delivery on your doorstep,"* then the mock-pity capitulation.
- **Pre-announce the joke, then deliver the anticlimax.** *"you're gonna laugh when I say what I'm gonna say because you're gonna be like, after that huge ramble about big opportunities, this is the thing... Let's talk about Grand Theft Auto six."* Telling the audience the joke is coming *is* the joke.
- **Anti-anticipation — refuse the buildup.** When Sam sets up a dramatic "write it down" bit: *"Tell me if it's done. Alright. It's done."* Killing the suspense by complying instantly.
- **Off-register / anachronistic framing.** Import vocabulary from the wrong domain: a 19th-century media baron gets a *"Tinder profile"*; reading tech-antitrust emails becomes *"These are my Epstein emails"*; a podcast deal is remembered like *"It sounded like 9/11."* The collision of registers is the laugh.
- **Recursive self-awareness — stack the cringe.** Given one embarrassing observation, he piles on more: *"they know for a fact that I'm not good enough to do it in one take... that I'm self conscious about it and felt like what I said was a little bit wrong and that I'm insecure and I wanted to do it better."* Multiplication of admissions.
- **Deadpan epithet as a noun.** *"It is not beyond mister free speech..."* (Elon). Sarcastic title delivered flatly, no explanation.
- **Literalize the idiom to concede a point.** Called out for missing red flags: *"the red flag is wrapped around my eyes."*
- **The specificity-rhythm "bit."** Not a one-liner but a slow build of concrete detail (the TBPN grind, the Poppy disadvantage pile) where the accumulation itself produces the laugh/awe.
- **Child-logic punchline.** *"They're like, you have to pay me $10 bucks, which is what they call money."* The parenthetical translation is the punch.
- **Heavy question, light register.** Close a cringe bit with a genuinely existential line delivered casually: *"honest question, is the loss of self respect worth the gain of influence and money?"*

**What makes them land:** the timing of the deflation (always one beat after the setup peaks), the flat delivery of the transgressive/self-critical part, and that the target is usually *himself*, which buys permission for everything else.

## 5. Sentence rhythm & verbal tics

- **Cadence:** conversational and run-on, with lots of self-interruption and mid-sentence restarts (*"What are we talking? Thirty — ten seconds? Thirty seconds?"*). He thinks out loud and lets you watch.
- **Repetition as rhythm:** doubles and triples words for beat and emphasis — *"No. No. No. Let me go — let me go see ground floor truth,"* *"vastly different... vastly different... vastly different,"* *"wow wow wow,"* *"Nobody. Nobody."*
- **Stacked short questions:** he asks one big question as a burst of small ones to give the answerer entry points — *"How do those conversations happen? How do you negotiate those deals? And why do they buy them for so much?"* and *"How do I simplify? How do I speak the language...?"*
- **Signature framing phrases:**
  - *"obviously... obviously..."* (used to wave past the expected answer before pivoting to the interesting one)
  - *"here's the case, let me make the case"* / *"let me phrase what I took away"* (permission-to-paraphrase before restating)
  - *"the [X] is the original [Y]"*, *"there's levels to this game"*, *"white belt to blue belt"*
  - *"it smells like..."*, *"that smells a little fishy"*
  - *"who cares?"*, *"it's okay. Whatever."*, *"like idiots,"* *"I guess"* (deflators)
  - *"I have a friend / a buddy of ours who's a brilliant marketer..."* (anecdote-as-standard-setting opener)
  - plain enthusiastic validators: *"That's an awesome story," "That's amazing," "Very valuable."*
- **Register control:** he'll deploy a $10-word idea and then immediately re-say it in $1 words (*"communicate upwards — what does that mean? How do I simplify?"*). The re-say in plain language is a constant habit.
- **Frame-announcing:** *"I'll do the personal story, and then I'll do the primer,"* *"let's spitball real quick,"* *"let me speculate for a second."* He tells you the structure before he executes it.

## 6. Do / Don't cheat-sheet

**DO**
- Take the biggest/scariest word in the sentence and say it in the tone of a chore.
- Map abstractions onto everyday images (movies, games, grocery aisles, fishing, kids).
- Do the homework and show it ("I was reading the research on you," holding up the book), then ask the guest to "brag."
- Be the audience surrogate — ask the honest dumb question out loud, trailing into disbelief ("...to have them?").
- Extract the system: name moves, restate what you heard, ask "what do you call that?"
- Stack concrete specifics and repeat keywords for rhythm instead of summarizing.
- Make *yourself* the cautionary tale / guinea pig before putting anyone else on the spot.
- Build a metaphor, ride it one beat too long, then flatten it ("...like idiots").
- Credit borrowed bits and questions openly — the honesty is the flex.
- Layer to the second-order insight instead of stopping at the obvious point.
- Drop plain, sincere validators ("That's an awesome story") — not every line needs to be clever.

**DON'T**
- Don't sound impressed by prestige or credentials; deflate them ("you don't need a 2,000 IQ").
- Don't defend yourself when corrected — pivot to amplify the story ("Oh, North of 2. Unbelievable exit.").
- Don't stay abstract — force the concrete ("which aisle?", throw out a "$10M" to get precision).
- Don't over-explain a joke or milk a buildup; sometimes refuse the anticipation entirely.
- Don't use corporate/jargon imagery for analogies; if a term is jargon, mock-confess you don't get it and make them unpack it.
- Don't hedge your self-criticism with a wink — deliver the unflattering truth straight.
- Don't let a great story just sit — name the principle it proves.
- Don't punch down; the recurring target of the sharpest jokes is himself.
