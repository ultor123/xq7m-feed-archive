# 7 Powers — Hamilton Helmer
> Distilled for speaking, pitching, and daily insight. Framework notes in own words.

## Core Thesis
Strategy is the study of what actually creates and sustains business value. Helmer's definition of a **Power**: a condition that creates *durable differential returns* — margins persistently above your cost of capital that competitors cannot arbitrage away. Every Power has two parts:
- **Benefit** — it improves cash flow (higher prices, lower costs, or lower investment needs).
- **Barrier** — the reason competitors *don't* copy it away, even when they can see it working.

No barrier, no Power. A benefit alone just invites imitation until returns collapse to average. The barrier is the whole game.

## The 7 Powers
1. **Scale Economies** — per-unit cost falls as volume rises. Barrier: a challenger would have to match your volume to match your cost, and can't without first winning the share they'd need the low cost to win. (Circular trap for the attacker.)
2. **Network Economies** — the product gets more valuable to each user as more users join. Barrier: a competitor starting from a smaller network offers a worse product at any given price. Winner-take-most dynamics.
3. **Counter-Positioning** — a newcomer adopts a superior business model that the incumbent *can't* copy without damaging its existing business. Barrier: **collateral damage** — the incumbent rationally chooses not to respond (copying would cannibalize their cash cow). This is the one incumbents lose to most predictably.
4. **Switching Costs** — the customer loses value if they move to a competitor (retraining, data migration, integration risk). Barrier: a rival must compensate the customer for that loss on top of beating you on merits.
5. **Branding** — durable attribution of higher value to an objectively identical offering (trust or identity). Barrier: takes years and consistent signaling to build; can't be bought quickly.
6. **Cornered Resource** — preferential access to a coveted asset (a patent, a key person, a deposit) on attractive terms. Barrier: you simply have it and they don't.
7. **Process Power** — embedded, hard-to-replicate ways of operating that yield superior product or cost (Toyota Production System). Barrier: **hysteresis** — it can only be built slowly through long organizational practice, not bought or reverse-engineered.

## Statics vs Dynamics
- **Statics** = *what* the Power is (the 7 above).
- **Dynamics** = *how you get it* — the origination path. Helmer's key claim: **Power is established during a period of change**, and its taproot is almost always **invention** (of product, business model, or process), not incremental optimization.

## Power Progression (which Powers are available when)
- **Origination stage** → Counter-Positioning, Cornered Resource (you're small; these don't need scale).
- **Takeoff stage** (rapid growth) → Scale Economies, Network Economies, Switching Costs, Branding get *established* here — this is the rare window.
- **Stability stage** → mostly Process Power is still buildable; the others must already be locked in.
The lesson: the moment to *establish* Power is the high-growth window. Miss it and the door closes.

## Kiln Applications
- **Sell Power, not activity.** When pitching, frame The Kiln's value as building a client's *barrier* (switching costs via embedded GTM infrastructure, process power in their pipeline), not "more meetings."
- **Counter-Positioning is the agency's own edge.** A lean, AI-native GTM shop can run a model legacy agencies can't copy without gutting their billable-hours business. Name that asymmetry explicitly.
- **Build switching costs into engagements.** The deeper The Kiln's systems embed into a client's data and workflow, the higher the barrier to churning — design for that from day one.
- **Diagnostic question for any strategy call:** "Where's the barrier?" If a client's advantage has a benefit but no barrier, it's temporary — say so.
