## Defense, Industry & Geopolitical History (From All-In: Stephens & Sankar)

### The American Industrial Base
> "The industrial base that won World War II was not a defense industrial base. It was an American industrial base." — Trey Stephens
- Chrysler: Minuteman ICBM + minivans
- General Mills: cereal R&D → torpedoes and inertial guidance
- Ford: built satellites until 1990
- In 1989, only 6% of defense spending went to pure-play defense specialists. Today it's 86%.

### The Founders of Defense Innovation
**Kelly Johnson (Skunk Works):** Built 41 airframes including the SR-71 (still fastest manned aircraft) and U-2 (still flying). One of his rules was playing defense to keep bureaucrats out.

**Bob Noyce (Fairchild/Intel):** In 1968, 96% of integrated circuits were sold to the Apollo program. He never let more than 4% of R&D be funded by government. Chased Moore's Law mercilessly. Result: when the military needed precision guided munitions in the 80s, the price-performance was already there.

**Admiral Rickover:** Built the nuclear navy against institutional resistance.

**Benny Shriber:** Drove the ICBM program — 8 competing programs at once. Today that would be called "wasteful." Then it was called "competition of ideas."

**Theodore Hall:** Youngest Manhattan Project scientist. Walked into the Soviet trade mission in 1944 and gave them bomb secrets because he thought bipolar nuclear power = world peace. "Every death from communism since 1949 is on his hands." — Sean Sankar. The ultimate cautionary tale of a technologist deciding they know better than democracy.

### The DJI Counterfactual
The drone is an American invention — Abe Kareem and the Predator, General Atomics. But ITAR classified drones as flying missiles, and the FAA banned beyond-line-of-sight operations. "There was a counterfactual world where General Atomics had a consumer subsidiary called DJI." — Sean Sankar. Regulation killed the domestic drone market and handed it to China.

### On America's Pattern
> "All periods of American greatness have started when we realize that we're the underdog. We were the 17th largest army in the world at the beginning of World War II. What did a ragtag bunch of farmers have taking on the world's largest army in the British during the Revolutionary War?" — Trey Stephens
