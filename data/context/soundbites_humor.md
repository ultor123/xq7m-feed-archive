# Humor, Wit & Funny Soundbites

## Business Humor — Lines That Kill on Podcasts

**On startups:**
> "A startup is just a group of people convinced they're about to change the world, who can't figure out how to fix the office printer." — adapted from Silicon Valley culture

> "Every startup deck says 'if we capture just 1% of the market...' and every VC knows that 1% is the hardest 1% you'll ever capture."

> "The three stages of a startup: 1) This is going to change everything. 2) Oh God, what have we done. 3) I can't believe that worked."

> "We pivoted. That's startup speak for 'the first idea was terrible but we can't admit that in the press release.'"

**On fundraising:**
> "A seed round is just paying for the privilege of finding out your idea doesn't work. A Series A is paying to find out it works but not the way you thought."

> "VCs want founders who are 'uniquely positioned to solve this problem.' Translation: have you already solved it and just need our money to do it louder?"

**Chamath — on VC:**
> "Most VCs are just well-dressed followers with a spreadsheet. They invest in what other VCs are investing in and call it 'pattern matching.'"

**Jason Calacanis — on Silicon Valley:**
> "San Francisco is the only city where you can be a billionaire and still complain about the rent."

**Shaan Puri — on tech culture:**
> "Tech Twitter is just people who made money telling people who haven't made money that they're thinking about making money wrong."

## Humor Frameworks (For Riffing)

**The Unexpected Escalation:**
Start with something relatable, then escalate to absurdity.
"Building a SaaS company is like cooking — you start with a recipe, realize you're missing half the ingredients, improvise wildly, and somehow end up in a Michelin-starred restaurant or a house fire. There's no in-between."

**The Deflationary Punchline:**
Build something up, then puncture it.
"We spent six months perfecting our go-to-market strategy, aligning every team, building the perfect funnel... and then our first customer came from a typo in a Reddit comment."

**The False Comparison:**
Compare two wildly different things.
"Raising a seed round is basically the same as online dating. You write a profile that's 70% aspirational, go on a bunch of awkward meetings, and eventually someone says yes for reasons you don't fully understand."

**The Irish Understatement:**
Say something dramatic in the most casual way possible.
"Yeah, we nearly went bankrupt in Q2. Grand, though. Sorted it."
This is the most Irish delivery possible and it kills every time because the casualness IS the joke.

## Podcast-Specific Openers

**If asked "tell us about yourself":**
> "I'm an Irish lad who moved to Portugal, went to MIT, and now I help companies figure out who to sell to. Which sounds simple until you realize most companies have no idea who they're actually selling to."

**If asked "what does The Kiln do":**
> "We're the plumbing behind the GTM machine. Nobody wants to talk about plumbing at a dinner party, but everyone notices when it doesn't work."

**If asked about AI:**
> "Look, everyone's either saying AI will save the world or destroy it. The truth is it's mostly being used to write slightly better cold emails. And honestly? That might save the world more than anyone realizes."

## Sharp Lines from All-In: Stephens & Sankar (Hill & Valley Forum)

> "Missiles and minivans. That's what Chrysler built. The Minuteman ICBM and the family car."
— Two words that make an entire industrial policy argument land instantly.

> "It'd be like accusing Excel of being a surveillance tool."
— Sean Sankar deflating the Palantir surveillance narrative. Perfect template for when someone mischaracterizes what you do.

> "That's tyranny by tech bro — a small number of people constraining the maneuver space of a democracy with no accountability."
— Sean on tech companies refusing to serve government. Flips the moral high ground completely.

> "David Packard's document was seven pages long. In the years since, it's become 2,000 pages. Did he screw us, or did the entropy just take hold?"
— Bureaucratic decay in one image. Works for any organization that lost its way.

> "I'm literally watching the guy, while I'm doing three miles over the speed limit, take heroin needles out of his arm next to an elementary school. And I'm like, totally fine."
— On getting a speeding ticket from an autonomous camera in San Francisco. The absurdity is the joke.

> "No one ever told me I could raise less at a lower price."
— From Silicon Valley (HBO), used by Trey as actual venture advice to defense startups. Kills every time because it's true.
