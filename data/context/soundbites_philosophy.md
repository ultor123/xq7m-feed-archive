# Philosophy, Stoicism & Mental Models

## Marcus Aurelius — Meditations

> "You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."

> "The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts."

> "Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one."

> "The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."
— This is the single most useful line in entrepreneurship. Every blocker is a feature, not a bug.

> "How much more grievous are the consequences of anger than the causes of it."

> "When you arise in the morning, think of what a precious privilege it is to be alive — to breathe, to think, to enjoy, to love."

> "The best revenge is not to be like your enemy."

> "Very little is needed to make a happy life; it is all within yourself, in your way of thinking."

> "If it is not right, do not do it. If it is not true, do not say it."

> "Never esteem anything as of advantage to you that will make you break your word or lose your self-respect."

> "The soul becomes dyed with the colour of its thoughts."

> "Accept the things to which fate binds you, and love the people with whom fate brings you together, and do so with all your heart."

## Seneca

> "It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a great deal of it."

> "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality."

> "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity."

> "He who is everywhere is nowhere."
— Incredible line for focus. When someone asks why you're not doing X, Y, Z.

> "It is not because things are difficult that we do not dare, it is because we do not dare that they are difficult."

> "Associate with people who are likely to improve you. Welcome those who you are capable of improving."

> "The whole future lies in uncertainty: live immediately."

> "Difficulties strengthen the mind, as labor does the body."

## Epictetus

> "It's not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters."

> "First say to yourself what you would be; and then do what you have to do."

> "No man is free who is not master of himself."

> "Make the best use of what is in your power, and take the rest as it happens."

> "If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish and stupid."
— Perfect for anyone building something contrarian.

## First Principles Thinking

**Elon Musk — on reasoning:**
> "I think it's important to reason from first principles rather than by analogy. The normal way we conduct our lives is we reason by analogy. We are doing this because it's like something else that was done, or it is like what other people are doing. First principles is a physics way of looking at the world. You boil things down to the most fundamental truths and then reason up from there."

**Richard Feynman — on understanding:**
> "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."

**Richard Feynman — on simplicity:**
> "If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough."

**Charlie Munger — on mental models:**
> "You've got to have models in your head. And you've got to array your experience — both vicarious and direct — on this latticework of models."

**Charlie Munger — on inversion:**
> "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there. Invert, always invert."

**Charlie Munger — on preparation:**
> "The game of life is the game of everlasting learning. At least it is if you want to win."

**Naval Ravikant — on leverage:**
> "Give me a lever long enough and a place to stand, and I will move the earth. Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage of the new rich."

**Naval Ravikant — on judgment:**
> "Earn with your mind, not your time. If you're still trading time for money, you haven't figured out leverage yet."

**Naval Ravikant — on reading:**
> "Read what you love until you love to read. The information diet is more important than the food diet."

**Naval Ravikant — on wealth:**
> "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep."

**Naval Ravikant — on specific knowledge:**
> "Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion rather than whatever is hot right now. It's the knowledge that you can't be trained for. If society can train you, it can train someone else and replace you."

## On Decision-Making

**Jeff Bezos — on two-way doors:**
> "Most decisions are two-way doors. You can walk through, look around, and if you don't like it, walk back. Treat them that way. The cost of being slow on reversible decisions is much higher than the cost of occasionally being wrong."

**Jeff Bezos — on disagreement:**
> "Have backbone; disagree and commit. If you have conviction on a particular direction even though there's no consensus, say 'Look, I know we disagree on this, but will you gamble with me on it?'"

**Jeff Bezos — on regret minimization:**
> "I knew that when I was 80, I was not going to regret having tried this. I was going to regret not having tried."

**Tim Ferriss — on fear-setting:**
> "We don't fear the unknown. We fear what we project onto the unknown. Define the worst case. You'll realize you can handle it."

**Ray Dalio — on radical transparency:**
> "Pain plus reflection equals progress. Most people avoid the pain. The ones who embrace it and reflect on it are the ones who grow fastest."

**Warren Buffett — on focus:**
> "The difference between successful people and really successful people is that really successful people say no to almost everything."
