# Crossing the Chasm — Geoffrey Moore
> Distilled for speaking, pitching, and daily insight. Framework notes in own words.

## Core Thesis
High-tech products don't fail because they're bad — they die in the gap between the customers who buy on vision and the customers who buy on proof. Moore's map is the **Technology Adoption Life Cycle**, and its central insight is that the cycle isn't smooth: there's a **chasm** where most promising products fall.

## The Adoption Life Cycle
Five groups, each buying for different reasons:
1. **Innovators / Techies** — pursue new tech for its own sake.
2. **Early Adopters / Visionaries** — buy on a dream; want a breakthrough advantage and will tolerate rough edges.
3. **Early Majority / Pragmatists** — buy on proven results; want the whole product, references from *other pragmatists*, and low risk.
4. **Late Majority / Conservatives** — buy only when it's a safe, established standard.
5. **Laggards** — resist until forced.

## The Chasm
The dangerous gap sits between **visionaries and pragmatists**. Visionaries want to be first and different; pragmatists want to be safe and want references from people like themselves. So the visionary reference is *worthless* to the pragmatist — the early wins don't transfer. Momentum stalls exactly when the company assumes it's about to take off.

## Crossing Strategy
- **Target a single beachhead niche.** Pick one narrow segment with a compelling, painful reason to buy *now*. Resist the temptation to stay broad. A big fish in a small pond beats a small fish in the ocean.
- **The D-Day analogy.** Concentrate overwhelming force on one narrow point of attack rather than spreading across the whole front.
- **Deliver the "whole product."** Pragmatists buy the complete solution to their problem, not the core technology — fill in the services, integrations, and support that make it turnkey.
- **Bowling pin → bowling alley.** Win the first niche completely, use it as a reference to knock down the adjacent segment, and let word-of-mouth among pragmatists cascade.
- **Then the tornado** — once pragmatists adopt as a herd, demand goes vertical and the game shifts to scaling and becoming the standard.

## Positioning for Pragmatists
- Lead with the problem and the segment, not the feature list.
- References must come from *within the target niche* — pragmatists trust their own kind.
- Name a clear competitor/alternative so the pragmatist can slot you on their mental map (an unpositioned product is a risk they won't take).

## Kiln Applications
- **Force the beachhead choice in every GTM plan.** The most common client mistake is targeting "everyone." Pick one segment with acute, present pain and dominate it before expanding — this is a core Kiln recommendation.
- **Build the reference engine.** Design outbound and case studies so early wins produce *same-segment* references — the only kind that moves pragmatist buyers.
- **Sell the whole product.** Position the client's offer as the complete solution to a specific job, not the clever core tech.
- **Diagnostic line:** "Who's your beachhead — the one narrow segment that will buy now and vouch for you to their peers? If it's 'everyone,' you're still stuck before the chasm."
