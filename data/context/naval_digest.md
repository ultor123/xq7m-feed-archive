## The How to Get Rich Thread (complete)

The May 31, 2018 tweetstorm, verbatim and in order — still his pinned tweet (~273K likes). [Original thread](https://x.com/naval/status/1002103360646823936) · [Full podcast series](https://nav.al/how-to-get-rich)

> 1. How to Get Rich (without getting lucky):
> 2. Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep. Money is how we transfer time and wealth. Status is your place in the social hierarchy.
> 3. Understand that ethical wealth creation is possible. If you secretly despise wealth, it will elude you.
> 4. Ignore people playing status games. They gain status by attacking people playing wealth creation games.
> 5. You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain your financial freedom.
> 6. You will get rich by giving society what it wants but does not yet know how to get. At scale.
> 7. Pick an industry where you can play long term games with long term people.
> 8. The Internet has massively broadened the possible space of careers. Most people haven't figured this out yet.
> 9. Play iterated games. All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest.
> 10. Pick business partners with high intelligence, energy, and, above all, integrity.
> 11. Don't partner with cynics and pessimists. Their beliefs are self-fulfilling.
> 12. Learn to sell. Learn to build. If you can do both, you will be unstoppable.
> 13. Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage.
> 14. Specific knowledge is knowledge that you cannot be trained for. If society can train you, it can train someone else, and replace you.
> 15. Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion rather than whatever is hot right now.
> 16. Building specific knowledge will feel like play to you but will look like work to others.
> 17. When specific knowledge is taught, it's through apprenticeships, not schools.
> 18. Specific knowledge is often highly technical or creative. It cannot be outsourced or automated.
> 19. Embrace accountability, and take business risks under your own name. Society will reward you with responsibility, equity, and leverage.
> 20. The most accountable people have singular, public, and risky brands: Oprah, Trump, Kanye, Elon.
> 21. "Give me a lever long enough, and a place to stand, and I will move the earth." — Archimedes
> 22. Fortunes require leverage. Business leverage comes from capital, people, and products with no marginal cost of replication (code and media).
> 23. Capital means money. To raise money, apply your specific knowledge, with accountability, and show resulting good judgment.
> 24. Labor means people working for you. It's the oldest and most fought-over form of leverage. Labor leverage will impress your parents, but don't waste your life chasing it.
> 25. Capital and labor are permissioned leverage. Everyone is chasing capital, but someone has to give it to you. Everyone is trying to lead, but someone has to follow you.
> 26. Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create software and media that works for you while you sleep.
> 27. An army of robots is freely available — it's just packed in data centers for heat and space efficiency. Use it.
> 28. If you can't code, write books and blogs, record videos and podcasts.
> 29. Leverage is a force multiplier for your judgement.
> 30. Judgement requires experience, but can be built faster by learning foundational skills.
> 31. There is no skill called "business." Avoid business magazines and business classes.
> 32. Study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers.
> 33. Reading is faster than listening. Doing is faster than watching.
> 34. You should be too busy to "do coffee," while still keeping an uncluttered calendar.
> 35. Set and enforce an aspirational personal hourly rate. If fixing a problem will save less than your hourly rate, ignore it. If outsourcing a task will cost less than your hourly rate, outsource it.
> 36. Work as hard as you can. Even though who you work with and what you work on are more important than how hard you work.
> 37. Become the best in the world at what you do. Keep redefining what you do until this is true.
> 38. There are no get rich quick schemes. That's just someone else getting rich off you.
> 39. Apply specific knowledge, with leverage, and eventually you will get what you deserve.
> 40. When you're finally wealthy, you'll realize that it wasn't what you were seeking in the first place. But that's for another day.

## Wealth & Leverage

Each tweet expanded by Naval himself on the podcast — the best paragraphs only.

### Seek wealth, not money or status

> Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep.

"You want wealth because it buys you freedom—so you don't have to wear a tie like a collar around your neck; so you don't have to wake up at 7:00 a.m. to rush to work and sit in commute traffic; so you don't have to waste your life grinding productive hours away into a soulless job that doesn't fulfill you."

"Wealth is not a zero-sum game. Everybody in the world can have a house. Because you have a house doesn't take away from my ability to have a house." — [nav.al/seek-wealth](https://nav.al/seek-wealth)

### You won't get rich renting out your time

> You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain your financial freedom.

"You really just want a job, or a career, or a profession where your inputs don't match your outputs. Businesses that have high creativity and high leverage tend to be ones where you could do an hour of work, and it can have a huge effect. Or you can do 1,000 hours of work, and it can have no effect." — [nav.al/renting-time](https://nav.al/renting-time)

### Earn with your mind, not your time

> Earn with your mind, not your time. — [Dec 2017](https://x.com/naval/status/944266790829408257)

### Specific knowledge

> Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion rather than whatever is hot right now.

"The first thing to notice about specific knowledge is that you can't be trained for it. If you can go to a class and learn specific knowledge, then somebody else can be trained for it too, and then we can mass-produce and mass-train people."

"If you're operating with 1,000 times leverage and somebody is right 80% of the time, and somebody else is right 90% of time, the person who's right 90% of the time will literally get paid hundreds of times more by the market." — [nav.al/specific-knowledge](https://nav.al/specific-knowledge)

### Permissionless leverage (code & media)

> Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create software and media that works for you while you sleep.

"The oldest form of leverage is labor… society overvalues labor as a form of leverage. This is why your parents are impressed when you get a promotion and you have lots of people working underneath you." — [nav.al/labor-capital](https://nav.al/labor-capital)

"The new generation's fortunes are all made through code or media… The beauty is when you combine all three: the minimum but highest-output labor (engineers, designers), capital for scaling, and code and media to get it all out there." — [nav.al/product-media](https://nav.al/product-media)

### Escape competition through authenticity

> You can escape competition through authenticity, when you realize that no one can compete with you on being you. — [Mar 2019](https://x.com/naval/status/1108874514111307778)

"If you are building and marketing something that's an extension of who you are, no one can compete with you. Who's going to compete with Joe Rogan or Scott Adams? It's impossible." — [nav.al/competition-authenticity](https://nav.al/competition-authenticity)

### Productize yourself

> Figure out what you're uniquely good at, and apply as much leverage as possible.

"You should ask yourself, 'Is this authentic to me? Is it myself that I'm projecting?' And then, 'Am I productizing it? Am I scaling it? Am I scaling with labor or capital or code or media?' It's a very handy, simple mnemonic." — [nav.al/productize-yourself](https://nav.al/productize-yourself)

### The four kinds of luck

"A lot of people think making money is about luck. It's not. It's about becoming the kind of person that makes money."

1. **Blind luck** — completely out of your control. Fortune, fate.
2. **Luck from hustling** — you generate enough force, hustle and energy that luck finds you.
3. **Luck from preparation** — you become very good at spotting luck when others can't see it.
4. **Luck from your unique character** — you build a unique brand and mindset, and luck seeks *you* out (the best deep-sea diver gets called when a treasure ship is found).

"Build your character in a way that luck becomes deterministic… your character becomes your destiny." — [nav.al/money-luck](https://nav.al/money-luck), [nav.al/luck-destiny](https://nav.al/luck-destiny)

### Live below your means

"Once you make a little bit of money, you still want to be living like your old self… don't run out to upgrade that house, and lifestyle. People busy upgrading their lifestyles just can't fathom this freedom." — [nav.al/salary-freedom](https://nav.al/salary-freedom)

### No get rich quick schemes

> There are no get rich quick schemes. That's just someone else getting rich off you.

"You don't want to learn how to be fit from a fat person. You don't want to learn how to be happy from a depressed person. And you don't want to learn how to be rich from somebody who makes their money by telling people how to be rich." — [nav.al/rich-quick](https://nav.al/rich-quick)

## Judgment & Decision-Making

### Judgment is the decisive skill

> In an age of permissionless leverage, judgement, not work, determines success and failure. Good judgement is the product of a calm and curious mind, reasoning without motivation and attachment. Whatever strengthens your ego weakens your judgement, and ultimately, your success.

"If you're steering Google or Apple, and your judgment is 10 or 20 percent better than the next person's, society will literally pay you hundreds of millions of dollars more, because you're steering a $100 billion ship."

"The thing that prevents you from seeing what's actually happening are your emotions. The people with the best judgment are actually among the least emotional." — [nav.al/judgment](https://nav.al/judgment)

### If you can't decide, the answer is no

> If you can't decide, the answer is no.

### Mental models worth knowing (his bonus episodes)

- **Principal-agent problem**: "If you're an employee, your most important job is to think like a principal. Train yourself how to think like a principal, and eventually you will become a principal." — [nav.al/principal-agent](https://nav.al/principal-agent)
- **Kelly criterion — avoid ruin**: "The number one way people get ruined in modern business is not by betting too much; it's by cutting corners and doing unethical or downright illegal things. A ruined reputation is the same as getting wiped to zero." — [nav.al/kelly-criterion](https://nav.al/kelly-criterion)
- **Net present value**: "The founder is negotiating based on what it's going to be worth in the future. You have to figure out what it's worth today by applying a discount rate… get very comfortable doing rough NPV calculations in your head." — [nav.al/npv](https://nav.al/npv)
- **Schelling points, price discrimination, consumer surplus** — [nav.al/schelling-point](https://nav.al/schelling-point), [nav.al/price-discrimination](https://nav.al/price-discrimination), [nav.al/surplus](https://nav.al/surplus)

### Reject most advice

> Most advice is people giving you their winning lottery ticket numbers.

"I use tweets to compress my own learnings. Your brain space is finite… You can think of these as pointers, mnemonics to help you remember deep-seated principles where you have the underlying experience to back it up." — [nav.al/reject-advice](https://nav.al/reject-advice)

### Truth & independent thinking

> Individuals search for truth, groups search for consensus. — [Jun 2019](https://twitter.com/naval/status/1141844787705741312)

> Guilt is society's voice speaking in your head. — [Sep 2017](https://twitter.com/naval/status/906906655144648704)

> If you eat, invest, and think according to what the 'news' advocates, you'll end up nutritionally, financially and morally bankrupt.

"Society does not just lie to you. It programs you to beat yourself up when you transgress one of its truths. Guilt is society programming you so effectively that you become your own warden." — [nav.al/consensus](https://nav.al/consensus)

## Happiness & Peace

### The core idea: happiness is a skill

> Happiness is a state where nothing is missing.

"The first step to increasing your level of happiness is realizing you can… Happiness is a skill like nutrition and fitness. It is not God-given. It's not handed to you and now you're stuck with it." — [nav.al/skill](https://nav.al/skill)

### Desire is a contract

> Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want.

"You become disturbed because you want something. Then you work really hard to get it and are miserable in the meantime. Finally, when you get it, you revert to the state you were in before you had it." — [nav.al/desire](https://nav.al/desire)

### Peace from mind

> We say peace of mind but what we really want is peace from mind. — [Jun 2018](https://twitter.com/naval/status/1005107581289824256)

"During your moments of greatest pleasure… your mind goes quiet. Deep down, what we're actually looking for is peace from mind."

"Here's a phrase I like: 'Peace is happiness at rest; happiness is peace in motion.' The ultimate goal is not happiness. The goal is peace." — [nav.al/peace](https://nav.al/peace), [nav.al/peace-motion](https://nav.al/peace-motion)

### The famous one

> A fit body, a calm mind, a house full of love. These things cannot be bought — they must be earned. — [Feb 2018](https://x.com/naval/status/966512979066765313)

Naval's own favorite of his tweets: "It's not necessarily the most insightful. But when I look at it, there's such a certain truth in there that it resonates." — [nav.al/finally-wealthy](https://nav.al/finally-wealthy)

### Being unhappy is inefficient

"A peaceful person doesn't have extraneous thoughts going through their head… When I got happier in my own life, I became much more effective—even though I don't work as hard as I used to. I make decisions much more clearly now." — [nav.al/inefficient](https://nav.al/inefficient)

### The modern struggle

> The modern devil is cheap dopamine. — [May 2020](https://x.com/naval/status/1265022431506251776)

> In an age of abundance, pursuing pleasure for its own sake creates addiction. — [Oct 2019](https://twitter.com/naval/status/1183731193516351491)

> The modern mind is overstimulated and the modern body is understimulated and overfed. Meditation, exercise, and fasting restore an ancient balance.

"The modern struggle is really about individuals—disconnected from their tribe, religion and cultural networks—who are trying to stand up to all these addictions that have been weaponized: alcohol, drugs, pornography, processed foods, news media, Internet, social media and video games." — [nav.al/addiction](https://nav.al/addiction)

### Smart people and happiness

> You're not smart because you're unhappy; you're unhappy because you're smart.

"Entrepreneurs will say, 'I don't want to be happy because I want to be successful.' But don't get it backwards… You can be happy and smart—it's just going to take more work. The good news is, smart people are good at figuring out the truth. The more you dig into certain deep truths, the freer and more peaceful you will become." — [nav.al/smart](https://nav.al/smart)

### Truth and inner silence

> The closer you are to the truth, the more silent you become inside. — [Jun 2019](https://twitter.com/naval/status/1141919795735961600)

> A rational person can find peace by cultivating indifference to things outside of their control. — [Oct 2017](https://x.com/naval/status/919317409852297218)

"If we want peace, we have to give up on self-conflict. We even have to give up on self-improvement, because self-improvement is just a dressed up form of self-conflict." — [nav.al/truth](https://nav.al/truth)

## Reading & Learning

> Read what you love until you love to read.

"I don't know a smart person who doesn't read and read all the time… It's not the means of education that are scarce. It's the *desire* to learn that's scarce."

"Instead of reading a business book, pick up Adam Smith's *The Wealth of Nations*. Instead of a book on evolution written today, pick up Darwin's *Origin of the Species*. Instead of advanced cosmology, pick up Feynman's *Six Easy Pieces*."

"The ultimate is when you walk into a library and you don't fear any book. You can take any book off the shelf, read it, absorb what is true, reject what is false." — [nav.al/love-read](https://nav.al/love-read)

> I don't want to read everything. I just want to read the 100 great books over and over again.

> Reading is faster than listening. Doing is faster than watching.

> Study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers. *(There is no skill called "business.")*

"The five most important skills are reading, writing, arithmetic, persuasion… and computer programming, because it's an applied form of arithmetic that gets you so much leverage for free in any domain." — [nav.al/math-logic](https://nav.al/math-logic)

> Acquiring knowledge is easy, the hard part is knowing what to apply and when. That's why all true learning is "on the job." Life is lived in the arena. — [2025](https://x.com/naval/status/1930058059172458665)

## Long-Term Games & Relationships

> Play long-term games with long-term people. All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest. — [Mar 2019](https://x.com/naval/status/1109704032204009473)

"Compound interest is a marvelous force… whether it's goodwill, or love, or relationships, or money. Long-term games are good not just for compound interest, they're also good for trust." — [nav.al/long-term](https://nav.al/long-term)

> Pick business partners with high intelligence, energy, and, above all, integrity.

"If you're trying to keep someone motivated for the long-term, that motivation has to come intrinsically. You can't just create it, nor can you be the crutch for them." — [nav.al/intelligence-energy-integrity](https://nav.al/intelligence-energy-integrity)

> Don't partner with cynics and pessimists. Their beliefs are self-fulfilling.

"The cynics and pessimists are basically saying: 'I've given up. And so why should you go do something? Because if you fail, then I'm right.'" — [nav.al/rational-optimists](https://nav.al/rational-optimists)

> Play stupid games, win stupid prizes.

"A lot of people spend their time playing social games like on Twitter where you're trying to improve your social standing and you basically win stupid social prizes which are worthless." — [nav.al/stupid-games](https://nav.al/stupid-games)

### Being ethical is long-term greedy

"You want to be honest because it leaves you with a clear mind. You don't want two threads running in your head, one with the lies you tell—and now have to keep track of—and the other with the truth."

"By being honest you're rejecting people who only want to hear pretty lies. Over the long-term you create room for the people who like you exactly the way that you are." — [nav.al/ethics](https://nav.al/ethics)

### Time management

> You should be too busy to "do coffee," while still keeping an uncluttered calendar.

"You have to be utterly ruthless about dodging meetings. When you do meetings, make them walking meetings… Nothing is getting done in a meeting with eight people around a conference table. You are literally dying one hour at a time." — [nav.al/meetings](https://nav.al/meetings)

"Set an aspirational hourly rate: If you can outsource something—or not do something—for less than your hourly rate, outsource it or don't do it." — [nav.al/hourly-rate](https://nav.al/hourly-rate)

### The AI Industrial Revolution (Jun 1, 2026) — flagship

~70-min episode with **Guillermo Rauch (Vercel), Blake Scholl (Boom Supersonic), Max Hodak (Science Corp)**, combining the 4-part late-May series. [nav.al/industrial](https://nav.al/industrial)

**Part 1 — Waste Tokens, Save Time** ([nav.al/tokens](https://nav.al/tokens), [his announcement thread](https://x.com/naval/status/2059812430633673045))
- "Just waste tokens, save time. Don't look at the tokens either as inputs or outputs. Just look at your time, and look at the final output." Models are "still way cheaper than a human" — throw Codex, Claude, and Gemini at the same problem to brute-force it.

- Models graduated from "junior engineers" to "principal engineers" — they return trade-off matrices unprompted.
- Naval: "I went from not having written code in twenty years to coding all the time now — through agents."
- Hodak: "you just don't get stuck anymore."
- Open question: "Is pure software dead?" Models speak English now — so where's the moat? Hardware?

**Part 2 — Vibe Coding Hardware** ([nav.al/hardware](https://nav.al/hardware), [thread](https://x.com/naval/status/2060200170936250463))

- "Humans are becoming verifiers." Code review becomes: "I understand the consequences of this PR. I'm signing off."

**Part 3 — The Regulatory Frontier** ([nav.al/regulatory](https://nav.al/regulatory))
- Regulatory "Red Queen race": "They're going to have agents, we're going to have agents… it's agent-on-agent wars."
- Boom prompts through 200 pages of lightning-strike compliance docs "in minutes, not months."
- Regulators' asymmetric incentives (Scholl): "If you approve a bad thing, your career is over. If you block a good thing, nobody notices."
- "Healthcare is a communist society inside capitalism." Naval's proposal: first 20% of annual income as healthcare deductible to create real price discovery.
- AI and crypto thrive "in the math domain, because that's the last unregulated domain."

**Part 4 — The Autonomous Company** — the ~20 min of new material in the combined episode.
