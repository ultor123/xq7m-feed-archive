# Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind — Al Ries & Jack Trout
> Distilled for speaking, pitching, and daily insight. Framework notes in own words.

## Core Thesis
Positioning is not something you do to a product — it's what you do to the **mind of the prospect**. You don't create a position by shouting features; you occupy a slot that already exists in the customer's head. In an overcommunicated society, the scarce resource is attention and memory, not information. The winning move is to be *simple, first, and different* in the mind.

## Key Principles
- **The mind is limited and hates confusion.** It defends against the flood of messages by accepting only what fits what it already knows. Complexity gets rejected. Oversimplify the message.
- **Be first.** The easiest position to own is the one nobody else has. The first brand into the mind gets the category-defining slot; being first beats being better.
- **If you can't be first, create a category you can be first in.** Redefine the frame so you lead a ladder of your own.
- **Own a word.** The strongest brands own a single word or attribute in the mind (safety, overnight, search). Trying to own several words dilutes all of them.
- **Ladders in the mind.** For each category the prospect holds a mental ladder with a few rungs. Know which rung you're on and strategize accordingly — you can't wish yourself to the top rung by advertising harder.

## Strategy by Position
- **Leader's strategy** — reinforce the original concept, cover challengers by embracing new approaches, and keep the category itself growing. The leader's danger is arrogance and ignoring flanking moves.
- **Follower's strategy** — don't attack the leader head-on. Find the **open hole**: an unowned attribute (size, price, gender, time of use, against-the-grain positioning). "The uncola" beats "a better cola."
- **Repositioning the competition.** Sometimes you make room by shifting how the prospect sees an entrenched rival, rather than promoting yourself.

## Traps
- **The line-extension trap.** Stretching a strong brand name across many products erodes the single clear idea it owned. A name that means one thing can't also mean everything.
- **The "everybody" trap.** Trying to appeal to all prospects and all uses positions you nowhere.
- **FWMTS ("Forgot What Made Them Successful").** Companies drift from the sharp idea that won them the mind in the first place.

## Kiln Applications
- **Pick one word for The Kiln.** Not "full-service GTM" — a single ownable idea (e.g. "GTM *infrastructure*"). Repetition of one word beats a feature list.
- **Position clients into open holes.** Most GTM work fails because the client competes head-on with a category leader. Find the unowned attribute and build the outbound narrative around it.
- **Resist line extension in messaging.** When a client wants the campaign to say five things, that's the confusion the mind rejects — force the choice of one.
- **Diagnostic line:** "What word do you own in your buyer's mind? If you can't answer in one word, neither can they."
